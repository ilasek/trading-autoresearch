## Mechanism

This is not a strategy; it is the licence (and the limits of the licence) for a step that appears
inside any learned candidate: **choosing a model or a hyperparameter by cross-validation on
serially dependent data.** The folder's existing coverage of this question is a single borrowed
practice — `notes/2026-08-29-machine-learning-cross-section-comparative.md` records Gu, Kelly &
Xiu using a fixed train/validation/test split with **"no cross-validation, to preserve temporal
ordering"** — and `research/SUMMARY.md` repeats it as if it were a derived rule. It is not. It is
a convention, and this paper says exactly when the convention is unnecessary and when it is
indispensable.

**The claim.** For a *purely autoregressive* model, ordinary K-fold cross-validation — folds
drawn at random, future rows used to fit a model evaluated on past rows — is **consistent for
prediction error**, provided the model's errors are serially uncorrelated. No purging, no
embargo, no blocking.

**The construction that makes this possible.** Embed the series into rows
`[y_{t-p}, …, y_{t-1} | y_t]` with a fixed order `p` and forecast horizon `h = 1`. K-fold CV then
leaves out **entire rows**, not individual observations. Provided the model is the true one,
`y_t − ĝ(x_t, θ̂)` is just a regression residual, and residuals are asymptotically i.i.d., so
dropping rows affects neither bias nor consistency. The serial dependence of the *series* has
been absorbed into the lagged predictors; what is left over is not dependent.

**The three assumptions, which are the whole content of the result** (the paper's A1–A3):

- **A1** — the data are generated by a **stationary, ergodic, nonlinear AR(p)** process of the
  form `y_t = g(x_t, θ) + ε_t`, with `x_t` the `p` own lags. "No misspecification is considered in
  our setup," they say plainly.
- **A2** — the leave-one-out estimator of `θ` is **consistent** (compact parameter space,
  `g` continuous and differentiable in `θ`, the objective converging uniformly to one with a
  unique minimiser).
- **A3** — the disturbances form a **martingale difference sequence**: serially uncorrelated
  (i.i.d. errors are a special case), conditionally homoskedastic with a finite `4+δ` moment, and
  absolutely continuous. Discrete-valued errors (`ε_t ∈ {−1, +1}`) explicitly fail A3.

**Theorem 1.** Under A1–A3, the CV estimate of prediction error converges in probability to the
true prediction error. `g` may be linear, nonlinear, parametric or effectively nonparametric —
splines, local polynomials and neural networks are all covered, because they are all
representable in that form.

**The failure path, and its direction.** Violate A1 (misspecify) and the residuals acquire serial
correlation; A3 then fails automatically, the key lemma collapses, and CV stops working. The
authors are specific about the direction: in that case CV **systematically underestimates the
error** — it is over-optimistic, and it overfits. Violate A2 and CV is invalid in the independent
case too, so that one is not a time-series issue at all.

**The detector is one line of code.** Because the whole result hinges on uncorrelated residuals,
the condition is directly testable: fit, then test the residuals for serial correlation with a
**Ljung–Box** test. If they are clean, K-fold is licensed; if they are not, the model is underfit
and both its CV estimate *and* the model are suspect.

**The counter-intuitive corollary, which is the practically useful part.** A model "sufficiently
large and flexible, and therefore liable to be overfitted" will *have* uncorrelated errors —
which is exactly the regime where cross-validation is most needed to control overfitting. So the
assumptions bind hardest on *underfit* models, and the usual folk rule ("never K-fold a time
series") is backwards about which models are at risk.

## Construction recipe

For a learned candidate that must pick a hyperparameter inside its visible window:

1. **Embed with a fixed lag order and `h = 1`**, one row per `(t)` — or, in a panel,
   per `(date, name)` — and treat the row as the atomic unit. Never split a row.
2. **Pick the hyperparameter by ordinary K-fold CV over those rows** (the paper's simulations use
   5-fold and leave-one-out; both beat a single hold-out, and leave-one-out is the least biased).
3. **Test the selected model's residuals for serial correlation (Ljung–Box).** Clean → the CV
   number is trustworthy. Correlated → discard it and treat the model as misspecified; do not
   "fix" the CV scheme, fix the model.
4. **Do not reach for h-block / "non-dependent" CV by default.** The paper measures it: dropping
   the `h` rows either side of each test row starves the fit, and it performed *considerably
   worse* than plain K-fold in every experiment where the model was adequate. Its only domain is
   the misspecified case, where a single out-of-sample block was better anyway.
5. **Prefer a single hold-out only when the model is known to be heavily misspecified.** That is
   the one experimental regime (their Experiment 3) where CV's precision advantage vanished and
   its bias dominated.

## Robustness evidence (qualitative only)

- **The theorem is algebra and cannot decay**, which is the strongest form of robustness this
  folder recognises; the appendix proof is present and checkable.
- **The simulations span three regimes** — correctly specified AR DGPs, MA DGPs (where any finite
  AR model is approximate), and deliberately heavily misspecified fits — with both linear models
  and neural networks as the learners. The pattern is stable: CV gives more precise error
  estimates than a single out-of-sample block when the fit is adequate, comparable bias, and both
  advantages disappear when the fit is bad.
- **What is thin.** Synthetic processes plus one non-financial real series; univariate throughout;
  `h = 1` throughout; stationarity assumed rather than tested; no cross-sectional dimension
  anywhere; no application to a trading or portfolio problem. 693 citations say the methods
  community has absorbed it, not that anyone has re-tested it on a financial panel.

## Implementability here

- **Apply the folder's own newest detector to this source before using it.** `SUMMARY.md`'s
  [2026-10-12] transferable output says to **read a method's stated scope before its results,
  because the word that names your binding constraint may not mean what your constraint is.**
  Run that test here and it bites immediately. This paper's "time series" means a **univariate,
  stationary, purely autoregressive process with one-step-ahead targets**. The lab's learned
  candidates are **cross-sectional panels**: roughly a thousand eligible names at each rebalance
  date, features that are not own-lags, and targets that are forward returns over a month. Three
  of A1's four words fail. Specifically:
  1. **Cross-sectional dependence at a date is outside the theorem entirely.** Returns of ~1,000
     names on one date are strongly contemporaneously correlated (the folder has an entire
     co-movement section on exactly this). A random K-fold over `(date, name)` rows puts some
     names of a date in the training fold and others in the test fold, and the resulting
     "out-of-fold" error is contaminated through that same-date correlation. Nothing in A1–A3
     covers it, and the direction of the resulting bias is the dangerous one: optimistic.
     **The admissible unit for a fold in a panel is the date, not the row.**
  2. **Overlapping targets violate A3 by construction.** A target of "return over the next 21
     trading days" sampled daily makes adjacent rows' errors mechanically correlated — the exact
     case the paper says breaks CV. This is the problem the finance ML tradition addresses with
     purging and embargoes, and it is *not* in tension with this paper: it is this paper's own
     stated failure mode, reached through the label rather than through misspecification.
  3. **Stationarity is assumed, not argued.** The lab's own `experiments/learnings.md` records
     that the validation window is not a homogeneous sample and that the two splits have opposite
     resolving power. A method premised on stationarity inherits that as a live risk.
- **So the usable, correctly scoped recommendation is narrow, and it is still worth having.**
  Where a candidate must choose a hyperparameter from data already released by the protocol's
  walk-forward bookkeeping (`CLAUDE.md`: "a training row is released only once its forward-return
  target was realized" — which is purging, already implemented and not the agent's to change),
  **K-fold over whole dates, with the folds drawn from released dates and the residuals checked
  for serial correlation, is a defensible and more precise alternative to carving a second
  hold-out block out of the training window.** It costs no extra data, and the paper's evidence is
  that it estimates error more precisely than the single-block alternative. Gu–Kelly–Xiu's "no
  cross-validation" is a convention they adopted, not a result they proved; it should be recorded
  in `SUMMARY.md` as such.
- **The one rule that transfers without qualification**, because it is about the lab's binding
  constraint rather than about CV: **a model whose residuals are serially correlated is underfit,
  and every error estimate taken from it is optimistic.** That is a free, pre-trial diagnostic of
  precisely the kind `experiments/learnings.md` already credits ("cheap holdings-only diagnostics
  keep killing ideas before they cost a trial"), it needs no validation read, and it applies to
  any candidate that fits anything — not only to learned ones.
- **What this does NOT license, stated so it cannot be misread.** It does not license K-fold over
  the protocol's own splits, any read of validation inside a candidate, or any use of rows whose
  targets were not yet realised. The engine's `causality_check` recomputes weights with the tail
  hidden and fails anything whose holdings move; nothing in this note changes that, and a
  candidate that "cross-validates" across the train/validation boundary is a protocol violation,
  not an application of this theorem.
- **Family placement.** `statistical-learning`, as a methodology note. It proposes no signal and
  cannot be a candidate; its value is that it removes one unexamined convention and replaces it
  with a condition plus a test.
