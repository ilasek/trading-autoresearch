## Top 3 learnings

1. **The null gate's bar, restated in the units candidates are actually judged in, is about half what
   the record carried — and it's predictable before the trial.** The gate `validation Sharpe >
   null_p90` is exactly `skill ≥ null_p90 − pool`. Measured on all 27 scored v3 trials, the bar in
   *skill* units averages **+0.030** (not the **+0.069** carried in edge-over-null-median units),
   because the null replicas inherit a candidate's own trading schedule and pay its costs — 57% of the
   old figure was construction drag, not a real hurdle. The bar also varies candidate-by-candidate
   over a **0.24-wide range** and falls predictably with a candidate's own turnover (R² 0.554, 26/27
   correct on held-out trials): `required skill = 0.086 − 0.054·ln(turnover)`. Composed with a small,
   uniform "tracking toll" every book pays (−0.018, from decomposing skill into an alpha and a
   beta/tracking channel), the gate becomes an information ratio requirement of **≈0.24** — a bar
   exactly one mechanism on the whole board (`price-trend`/M3) has ever cleared, and the best
   non-price-trend reading anywhere is a factor of **1.2**, not six, away from it.

2. **The lab's one live lead is thinner than its own record implied.** `M3`'s score needs ~108 months
   of a name's own history, so on this panel it has **zero scorable names before 1999** and runs on a
   23–36-name, nearly all-US book through 2003; 82% of its 21-year gross drift sits in 2004–2008. Once
   the lab calibrated a persistence-matched null for its primary screen for the first time (this
   week), the apparent full-window agreement across two independent instruments (z +3.3 to +3.9) did
   **not** survive a sub-window split: z **+4.18** on the thin 1997–2008 sample, **−2.09** on
   2009–2012, +2.91 on 2013–2017, netting **+0.18 controlled** over 2009–2017 as a whole — the only
   stretch with a real cross-section. The lead is not refuted, but it is sign-unstable outside its
   oldest, thinnest sample.

3. **Two of the lab's own instruments were miscalibrated and are now fixed.** The standing bps screen
   — the instrument behind most of `learnings.md` — reads information-free scores as *positive*, in
   proportion to their rank correlation with volatility (+56 to +512 bps as that correlation runs 0.04
   to 1.00 at h252), because it averages simple returns against a Sharpe gate; moving to log1p returns
   repairs it. Applying the fix to `M3` costs it 9–14% of its edge with no change in significance, so
   `M3` survives as a genuine first-moment effect even after the correction — but a separate reading
   this week (`amihud126`, +1036 bps, t +2.80) was shown to be the lab's known back-adjustment
   contaminant surfacing a fourth time, and inverted to −99 bps, t −0.66, once the mandatory
   within-region log-price control was applied in the right order. Control *ordering* is load-bearing,
   not optional.

  stated condition for the next candidate, both came back null on both signs; the inversion the lab

  correlation, not of slow objects generally.
- A stray remote branch (`origin/main-a5dglb`) was investigated during this session: its one commit is
  patch-equivalent to content already on `main` and touches no trial, leaderboard or champion file —
  unlike the August 2026 split-history incident, no trial data is at risk and the deflated-Sharpe bar
  is not understated. No action was needed beyond this note.

## Suggested focus for next week

With the gate now legible in skill units and composed into an information-ratio target (≈0.24, and a
factor of ~1.2 away from the best non-price-trend reading), the most direct next step is a second
mechanism aimed squarely at that number, in a family that still has none of program.md's required
per-family trials. Settling F3 — whether turnover genuinely buys a cheaper gate net of its own cost —
needs one clean paired measurement at a wide turnover spread (1.6x vs ~8x on the same score), which is
free and was named but not yet built. And given the M3 sub-window result, any new `price-trend`
candidate should report its own train-skill split by era rather than relying on the full-window
figure.
