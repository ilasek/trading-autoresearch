## Top 3 learnings

1. **A third, genuinely cheaper way to trade the lab's one working signal was found this week —
   and then the lab proved, on its own numbers, exactly how much of it is real.** Holding the
   ten-year reversal's informative half in six overlapping monthly batches instead of rebuilding
   it every month cuts trading by about 45% and posted v3's best-ever validation skill (+0.124,
   trial #16). A follow-up decomposition split the gain into a "trade less, pay less" piece and a
   "averaging lagged picks reduces noise" piece — and a second, independent test on an unrelated
   signal (the liquidity-volume trend, trials #18/#19) confirmed only the first piece transfers
   from train to validation at a predictable size (~+0.011 of skill); the "noise reduction" piece
   does not generalize and is object-specific. This is the first construction trick in the lab's
   v3 history with a measured, repeatable transfer — but it is a cheaper way to trade an existing
   bet, not a new edge, and it is nearly fully priced in now.
2. **The promotion gate is mathematically out of reach at this panel's signal strength — not "hard," but arithmetically closed.** Clearing the promotion bar (deflated-skill probability ≥ 0.95) needs
   a validation skill of +0.40 to +0.55 depending on a trial's own precision, against a board-best
   of +0.124 ever recorded. Converted into the lab's own signal units, that needs a stock-ranking
   correlation 3x the best this lab has ever measured at the most generous read, and 8–10x at the
   typical one — the single best signal ever measured here (+0.041) still lost money after costs.
   A second, related finding this week closes the obvious workaround: combining two
   lightly-correlated winning strategies into one book does NOT help clear the bar, because below
   where every one of this lab's results currently sits, the statistical gate actually rewards a
   *wider* error bar, not a narrower one — so diversifying a bet backfires on this exact test.
3. **A designed, twelve-way side-by-side experiment gave the cleanest proof yet that a good-looking training-data result is not a validation result.** Two near-identical strategies, picked
   in advance to test opposite calendar-based theories, ranked first and last out of twelve on
   training data (a strong, pre-announced result) — and then ranked in exactly the opposite order
   when checked on the untouched validation years. Across all 19 of this year's new-protocol
   trials, the correlation between a strategy's training-data result and its validation result is
   essentially zero. Separately, the lab also caught and neutralized a new data-quality trap:
   stocks with longer price history in the database score well for reasons that have nothing to
   do with the market — it is an artifact of when international stocks were added to the data
   vendor's coverage, not a real trading signal.

## Notable failures worth remembering

- **Nineteen straight v3 trials, none within an order of magnitude of the promotion bar.** The
  board's best deflated-skill probability (0.161) is about one-sixth of the 0.95 required, and
  this week's own arithmetic says no construction trick can close a gap that size — only a
  genuinely new, much stronger signal could, and none has been found in any family tried.
  `price-trend`, `liquidity-volume`, `statistical-learning`, `statistical-arbitrage` and
  `lead-lag-spillover` are now closed on direct measurement; `seasonality-calendar` and the
  risk/volatility axis are closed on execution-cost arithmetic. No family audited this week has an
  unmeasured reason left to try again.
- **A paired control built on purpose to fail a calendar-timing theory instead posted the best
  historical null-percentile score on the whole v3 board (100th), purely as a one-in-twelve
  statistical accident of which comparison it happened to be run as.** The journal explicitly
  flags it as not a result to build on; it is recorded as an anti-candidate precisely because
  acting on it would repeat a mistake the lab has already caught itself making once before
  (choosing a result because the number came out well, rather than because it was predicted).
- **A two-trial branch of work went unmerged for about a day**, which meant the published trial
  count (and therefore the statistical bar every later trial is judged against) was briefly wrong
  by two records. No experiment ran during the gap, so nothing was mis-graded, but it cost one
  session's budget to diagnose and the next session's to fix. Housekeeping flagged but not acted
  on: ten now-fully-merged branches are safe for a human to delete.

## Suggested focus for next week

1. **The challenge/promotion track should not be the target of any new trial until a materially
   stronger signal appears.** This week's own math shows why: no construction trick available to
   this lab can close an 8–10x gap in signal strength. Budget belongs entirely on the scout track,
   which — a secondary finding this week — is actually *cheaper* under the current protocol than
   the lab's older cost table implied, not more expensive.
2. **No family currently has an un-mined, measurement-free reason to probe again**; this week's
   audit found every closed family's closure rests on either direct measurement or settled
   execution-cost arithmetic, not just on trials run out. The honest next step may be more research
   (new data, new signal families) rather than more trials inside the existing ones.
3. **A growing pile of items need a human decision, not another trial**: a known pricing-data
   contaminant in raw price/volume levels (flagged twice already), a bias in how the statistical
   null is built, the newly found price-history-length data artifact, a tension between "always
   ship a paired control" and "every trial raises the statistical bar" (the two can work against
   each other), and the fact that the lab's own ranking script prints a statistic that isn't
   actually comparable from one trial to the next. None of these block research, but all of them
   are now load-bearing for how past and future results should be read.
4. **Housekeeping, not urgent:** ten now-fully-merged `origin/main-*` branches are safe to delete,
   which would stop every session's integrity check from having to re-clear them.

## Permanent caveat

This research runs on survivorship-biased, free end-of-day data with modeled (not live)
execution and costs. It is research output, not investment advice.
