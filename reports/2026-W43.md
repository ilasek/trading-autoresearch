## Top 3 learnings

1. **The stored price and volume *levels* carry a corporate-action lookahead that neither
   existing guard can see, and it explains six of the lab's recorded characteristics.** The
   data store keeps each name's bars adjusted as of the day they were *fetched*, so a stored
   close at date t is the raw price times every split/dividend factor applied **after** t. That
   factor cancels in every ratio, return or trend — the normal way a strategy uses price — but
   dominates a bare price or volume *level*, which is invisible to `causality_check` (the stored
   numbers don't change when the tail is hidden, so a level score recomputes bit-identically)
   and to the hindsight guard (a level names no instrument). Two free, non-economic signatures
   confirm it's a leak rather than a real effect: the IC scales with years of store remaining
   after the decision date (~0.0043/year, monotone across four buckets) and grows like
   √horizon out to 504 days, the opposite of how a real forecast decays. It changes no recorded
   verdict — no trial here has ever scored on a bare level — but it reframes six characteristics
   (the volatility family, the liquidity family, 52-week-high proximity) as collapsing onto this
   contaminated level rather than onto a genuine volatility axis, and because the leak is about
   twice as strong on train as on validation, it's a mechanical contributor to this lab's
   long-standing "a clean train screen is not evidence" pattern. Filed as an `## Engine issue`
   for human review, with an interim operating rule adopted meanwhile: a candidate may use
   ratios, returns and trends of the stored panels, never their raw levels, and may not
   orthogonalise against a level either.
2. **The lab now has a one-sided exchange rate between a rank IC and a Sharpe, calibrated this
   week: train skill ≈ 3.37 × within-region rank IC, with a noise floor around sd 0.025.** It
   immediately priced twenty of twenty-two free-screened objects out before anyone would have
   written a candidate for them. The caveat that makes it usable rather than misleading: the
   realised-to-predicted ratio across seven objects ran from 6% to 38% and was negative once,
   because a rank IC is a whole-cross-section statistic and a top-K book is a tail statistic —
   so the exchange rate is a **ceiling**, good for refusing a candidate before it's written,
   never for promising one will clear the bar.
3. **A bps-based screen and the gate's own Sharpe-skill statistic can disagree about the
   *sign*, and disagree most exactly where the bps number looks biggest.** Across eight
   measured objects, every case with a large bps excess and a volatility ratio at least 0.14
   away from 1.0 turned out to have a negative or noise-floor Sharpe skill — e.g. one object read
   +53.6 bps/63d (t +2.17) but realised -0.041 of Sharpe. Nearly every free-measurement block in
   this journal before this week was read in bps, so this isn't one caution anymore, it's a
   standing instruction: report a slice's volatility ratio alongside any bps excess, or don't
   trust the sign.
