# Weekly Report — 2026-W41 (Oct 4–10)

> **How to read this report.** It summarises a **re-scoring**, not new research. Every strategy
> the lab has ever recorded (104 under protocol v1, 6 under v2), plus the eight ported v1
> champions and three sanity strategies, was re-run under **protocol v3**
> (`reports/protocol-v3-methodology.md`; in PR #7, not yet merged). Train and validation only:
> **no holdout data was read**, and nothing was added to `experiments/trials.jsonl`. Raw results:
> `experiments/protocol_v3/rescore.jsonl`; harness: `scripts/rescore_protocol_v3.py`.

**TL;DR.** Under realistic execution, **no strategy the lab has built shows skill it can prove**.
"Skill" here means beating a simple equal-weight basket of every eligible stock.
- **Unconfined trials:** none of the 82 has a skill interval above zero. The best is +0.12 of
  Sharpe, with a 90% interval of about ±0.55.
- **The three that do:** all three trials with an interval above zero are confined to today's
  surviving stocks, and one of them is a placebo that picks stocks at random.
- **Train period:** only 5 of the 82 also beat the basket over 1997–2017.
- **The board itself:** its order cannot be resolved. All 82 unconfined trials sit in the set
  the data cannot separate from the best, and 60 of them are one bet (momentum) under several
  family labels.
- **This week's lead:** the residual-reversion strategy that led the v2 board on Oct 7 scores
  zero skill and loses at higher costs.
- **Next:** the nightly loop is paused until PR #7 is merged.

## Top 3 learnings

1. **No mechanism in the lab's history has demonstrated skill against the equal-weight basket.**
   Out of 82 unconfined trials, 0 have an interval above zero and 5 have positive train skill.
   The best deflated-skill probability is about 0.29 against a 0.95 bar (illustrative: computed
   from interval-implied standard errors). The honest summary of
   ten weeks of search is "indistinguishable from holding the basket", not "momentum works".
2. **The board cannot be ranked, and the families are fewer than they look.** All 82 trials sit
   in one confidence set, and 74 of them are two mechanisms. A list ordered by validation
   Sharpe had no resolving power under v1 either: 101 of 103 v1 trials were in the confidence
   set of v1's own best.
3. **The v1 champion ladder was fitting noise.** Under v3, each of the five promotions after
   #42 lowered validation Sharpe (0.39 → 0.25 → 0.24 → 0.23 → 0.12). The validation window had
   been used for selection so many times that improvements on it stopped carrying over to
   anything else.

## Notable failures worth remembering

- **The Oct 7 "tradeable residual-reversion premium".** It was the old execution model: fills
  at the signal's own close (bid-ask bounce on the last day of a 21-day reversal), free daily
  rebalancing, and a flat 15 bps on a book tilted toward illiquid, 71%-non-US,
  stamp-duty-paying names. It still beats random books built like it (98th–100th percentile)
  because random churn pays the same costs, but it does not beat simply holding the basket.
- **98 of 109 trials fail the new train gate.** Train never supported most of what validation
  appeared to show.
- **A no-information placebo outranks every real unconfined strategy.** That is the clearest
  measure yet of what picking from survivors is worth.

**TL;DR.** No strategy holds the v3 champion seat — the full v1 ladder re-scored above shows

to 112. The week's real finding is structural rather than a strategy: a cheap, low-turnover
corner of v3's cost model is now measured and open, but neither mechanism tried in it beat the
equal-weight eligible pool.

**Champion & trend.** Still no seat under v2/v3 (`strategies/champion_card.json` is unchanged

**Top 3 learnings.**
1. The v3 cost model's cheap corner (large book, low turnover) is real and nearly free
   (~14 bps/yr) — but Sharpe is flat in beta on this panel, so the low-volatility selection that
   tested it earned less return in exact proportion to its lower risk and scored no skill.
2. Residual reversion's gross alpha is the same size as its own v3 cost bill: a 63-day
   reformulation with a no-trade band still came in at -0.001 train skill (a 21-year dead heat),
   and restricting it to liquid names deletes 91% of the gross excess. The mechanism is closed,
   not just its original 22x-turnover construction.
3. The no-trade retention band itself is the week's one genuine construction win — holding a
   name while it stays inside a wider band cut turnover 42% while *raising* excess return — and
   is worth carrying onto any future signal, independent of which signal failed this week.

**Notable failures worth remembering.** Infrastructure, not strategy, cost the lab the most
time this week: a completed data-refresh run (seeding the T-bill rate) was silently lost to a
failed `git push` (HTTP 408) and had to be re-run from scratch, and a merged PR left orphaned
commits on its source branch that didn't land until a human opened a fresh PR a day later.
Separately, four `tests/test_membership.py` assertions (FTSE 100 / N225 / SMI / HSI change-log
bookkeeping) are currently red on `main`; verified not to touch train/validation eligibility,
but still needs a human fix.

**Suggested focus for next week.**
1. Human: clear the four red `test_membership.py` assertions so the engine suite is fully
   green again.
2. Keep testing the now-measured cheap/low-turnover corner, but with a mechanism that isn't a
