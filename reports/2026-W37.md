## Top 3 learnings

1. **The six-tranche overlap's validation-side justification was mis-attributed for ~20 trials — it buys breadth, not fresher information — but this changes nothing about the seat.** Two matched-breadth K=1 scouts (49.2 and 62.5 names, the latter pinned to the champion's own 62.69) decompose the champion's −0.109 validation gap to the best narrow challenger: **89% is the plain price of holding more names, 11% is the vintage-freshness residual, and that residual is statistically indistinguishable from zero** (fit R²=0.975 on Sharpe vs. book size alone). The lab is explicit that this is a **validation-only** statement: the human's 2026-08-26 rollback to K=6 was made on the *holdout* (K=6: 1.292; the four K=1-ward successors collapsed 1.377→0.691 there), and no trial this week read holdout. Nothing here licenses a K=1 challenger.
2. **`portfolio-learning` is closed on all three ways of combining two signals into one book.** Following up on the mean (bounded) and max (content-blind) operators closed earlier, this week's intersection operator was shown to respond to leg content (unlike max) but still can't beat its own better leg even after fixing a breadth-scaling confound — eleven trials, three operators, three failures. Recommendation: stop proposing combiners over these legs.
3. **A new pre-trial "depth profile" screen — each score's marginal excess by rank slice — passed its first two prospective tests.** It predicted, before either candidate was written, that narrowing `seasonality-calendar`'s inherited band would help (it did: 0.782→0.846) and that widening it would hurt (it did: 0.782→0.705) — the opposite sign to the `liquidity-volume` bracket the rule was originally fitted on. This is now a standard pre-registration step: profile the score, print a placebo alongside it, before choosing any band.

## Notable failures worth remembering

- **The parametric portfolio policy's headline claim collapsed for free, after a promising trial.** A weights-are-affine-in-characteristics construction (no ranking, no band) scored validation Sharpe **0.875** — the second-best non-`price-trend` result ever — but a same-night measurement showed its advertised "breadth chosen by the utility, not a band" is false: the long-only clip creates a hard arithmetic floor near half the pool that no rescaling can go below. A follow-up candidate built to pin breadth tighter was written, found to emit zero valid rows on validation, and deleted without being scored.
- **An excess screen predicted a gain from dropping ETFs out of the liquidity-volume book (+2.14pp/yr); the actual trial reversed sign.** The mechanism argument (ILLIQ mis-describes ETFs) still stands, but the score-level screen was blind to a variance channel the construction also moved — a reminder to check a book's volatility percentile alongside any tail-excess prediction.
- **A "kill switch" robustness test (Henriksson–Merton) gave a false green light on a mechanism the lab already refuted** (the 200-day trend-switch overlay). It's now used only to kill candidates, never to confirm them.
- **`range-variance` remains the only family with zero recorded trials, for the eleventh session running**, now backed by fifteen screened mechanisms and four independent statistics all pointing at the same survivorship-driven volatility artifact as the reason nothing there is tradeable. This still needs a human decision: amend `program.md`'s cold-family rule or formally retire the family.

# Re-scoring note — 2026-10-05 (protocol v2)

Everything above was measured under protocol v1, on today's ~140 constituents — survivors by
construction. The protocol-v2 re-scoring (merged 2026-10-05, ilasek/trading-autoresearch#4) re-ran
all 104 recorded trials on a point-in-time universe, where a strategy sees a name only while it was
a member of its index (S&P 500 from 1996; eight European and Asian indices from 2009). Across all
trials, v1 and v2 validation rankings agree at Spearman +0.43, none of v1's ten best trials stays in
v2's top ten, and the median validation Sharpe falls from 0.88 to 0.49. Full results:
`reports/protocol-v2-comparison.md` and `reports/protocol-v2-survivorship.md`.

The permanent caveat at the end of the body is therefore no longer hypothetical: the survivorship
bias it describes has now been measured, and for the strategies named in this report it is large.
