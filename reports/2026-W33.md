## Top 3 learnings

1. **The daily vol-spike trim's benefit is specific to the magnitude-weighted basket, not concentrated momentum baskets in general.** Applying the identical daily-reacting trim to the plain equal-weight buffered 12-1 basket (`mom_12m_daily_volspike_trim`) was a near no-op — Sharpe, maxDD, and turnover all landed within rounding of the untrimmed basket, because that basket's own realized vol rarely crosses the 1.6x spike threshold. This closes the "does the trim generalize?" question from last week's suggested focus.
2. **52-week-high proximity is refuted as a return-magnitude substitute, and not close.** Swapping the composite z-score signal for nearness-to-52-week-high (same buffer/weighting mechanism) collapsed validation Sharpe to 0.35 and more than doubled turnover (7.0x → 14.6x) — the bounded (0,1] ratio clusters names near 1.0 in bull markets, so small price wiggles flip buffer-band membership far more than an unbounded z-scored return does. The "new signal source" direction isn't fully closed, but this specific naive construction is.
3. **Stopping deliberately beats forcing a weak trial.** With no third hypothesis carrying a strong-enough rationale (blending reversal back in, further trim-tuning, and regime filtering are all already closed), the session ran only 2 of its 8-trial budget rather than spend budget — and thus raise the DSR bar — on a low-conviction idea. This is the second session to do this (also seen 2026-08-07).

## Notable failures worth remembering

- Both of this week's trials were negative results that closed open questions cleanly rather than novel failures — no new bug or methodology surprise this week (contrast with last week's sparse-weight zero-fill bug in trial #26).
- The core tension flagged last week persists and sharpened: the strongest challenger's DSR score is now measured against a stale, lower trial count. Every trial spent probing incremental variants — rather than a structurally new idea — makes the eventual promotion bar harder to clear even if Sharpe holds steady.

## Suggested focus for next week

1. A structurally different idea layered on `mom_zscore_narrow_daily_volspike_trim` (val Sharpe 1.07, last-known DSR 0.9326) remains the most promising path to clearing 0.95 — not another trim/threshold tweak or another signal source inside the same weighting mechanism, both now closed.
2. If no such idea has strong rationale, consider deliberately re-running the best challenger once to get a current DSR reading against the 31-trial bar, rather than letting it go stale indefinitely.
3. The "genuinely new signal source" avenue is still open in principle (a fixed-up 52-week-high proximity with an explicit turnover fix, or an entirely different family) — 31 trials have now explored buffering, weighting, blending, diversification, and risk overlays fairly exhaustively within the momentum family.

# Correction — 2026-08-16

Everything above was written from `main`'s history alone. It was accurate to what
`main` recorded and wrong about the week. From 2026-08-12 to 08-15 the nightly
routine pushed to per-run branches instead of `main`, so four sessions and **19
trials** were invisible when this report was compiled. Full detail is in the
"Protocol issue — 2026-08-16" entry in `experiments/journal.md`.

## What the report got wrong

| Claim above | Actually |
|---|---|
| "**2 trials** run in a single nightly session" | **21 trials** across **5 sessions** (2 on `main` 08-11, 19 off-branch 08-12..15) |
| "Cumulative trial count: **31**" | **50** strategies actually evaluated to date |
| "A quiet week — mostly confirming boundaries" | Four nights produced the best results in the repo's history |
| Best challenger `mom_zscore_narrow_daily_volspike_trim` (1.066) | Superseded twice — see below |
| "No sessions ran Aug 12, 13, 14, or 15" | All four ran; their output never reached `main` |

## What was actually found (off-branch, unverified on `main`)

| Session | Best candidate | Val Sharpe | maxDD | Turnover |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 08-12 | `mom_zscore_daily_voltarget` | 1.073 | -30.7% | 8.08x |
| 08-13 | `mom_zscore_weightband_trim` | 1.058 | -30.6% | 6.79x |
| 08-14 | `mom_zscore_fixed_anchor` | 1.085 | **-28.2%** | **5.75x** |
| 08-15 | `mom_zscore_overlap6_daily_trim` | **1.107** | -29.1% | **3.01x** |

The 08-14 session also reported that roughly half the re-sizing turnover in every
magnitude-weighted candidate since trial #20 came from the weight vector
anchoring to the swapped-out marginal member rather than from signal. If it
holds, that affects trial #28 — `main`'s best challenger — in the direction of
*understating* it.

## Why these numbers cannot be acted on

Each session computed its deflated-Sharpe bar by reading a `trials.jsonl` missing
the other 19 records: all four used an annualised sr_max of **0.4863** when the
true attempted history gives **0.5012**. Every DSR recorded in those sessions is
therefore measured against too low a benchmark and is overstated. Corrected
values cannot be derived from the files alone — DSR needs each candidate's daily
return series, which only `run_experiment.py` can regenerate.

The 19 trials were deliberately **not** imported into `main`: doing so would
require hand-editing a file CLAUDE.md freezes to `run_experiment.py`. They are
preserved as annotated tags `archive/nightly-2026-08-12` .. `-15`. Inspect with:

    git show archive/nightly-2026-08-14:experiments/journal.md

**Nothing in this correction may be treated as an established result.** Any idea
worth pursuing must be re-run through `run_experiment.py` against `main`'s trial
history so it is scored on an honest bar.

**Permanent caveat (unchanged):** survivorship-biased free daily price data,
costs at 15 bps/side. Research output, not investment advice.

# Re-scoring note — 2026-10-05 (protocol v2)

Everything above was measured under protocol v1, on today's ~140 constituents — survivors by
construction. The protocol-v2 re-scoring (merged 2026-10-05, ilasek/trading-autoresearch#4) re-ran
all 104 recorded trials on a point-in-time universe, where a strategy sees a name only while it was
a member of its index (S&P 500 from 1996; eight European and Asian indices from 2009). Across all
trials, v1 and v2 validation rankings agree at Spearman +0.43, none of v1's ten best trials stays in
v2's top ten, and the median validation Sharpe falls from 0.88 to 0.49. Full results:
`reports/protocol-v2-comparison.md` and `reports/protocol-v2-survivorship.md`.

The permanent caveat at the end of the body is therefore no longer hypothetical: the survivorship
bias it describes has now been measured, and for the strategies named in this report it is large.
