## Top 3 learnings

1. **Overlay reaction speed must match the speed of what it's trying to catch.** A basket-own realized-vol-spike trim (21d/252d vol ratio) was a near no-op evaluated monthly (fired twice in 6 validation years, zero drawdown improvement) but became the single best risk-reduction lever in the repo once re-evaluated daily — cutting validation maxDD from -36.0% to -29.5%/-30.3% while *raising* Sharpe to a new best of 1.05–1.07. Every earlier de-risking overlay (external trend/regime signals) failed from whipsaw; this one failed from lag until cadence was fixed. General lesson for future overlay ideas: check reaction speed vs. rebalance cadence before concluding a mechanism doesn't work.
2. **Within-basket weighting direction matters as much as membership.** Tilting capital *toward* the strongest-momentum names (rank-weight → z-score-magnitude-weight → multi-horizon z-score) lifted validation Sharpe from 0.90 to 1.03 in three isolated steps — the mirror image of every refuted low-vol/inverse-vol attempt, which tilted away from strong names and lost Sharpe. This is now a well-established, productive lever.
3. **Capital dilution carries a roughly constant Sharpe tax regardless of the base leg's quality**, and de-risking via *switching/blending* (external trend filters, regime gates, hedge-asset redirects, ETF sleeve blends) reliably backfires out-of-sample even when it looks great in-sample — three separate overlay families and a TLT/GLD hedge-redirect attempt all confirmed this. Diversification-axis fixes (sector-neutral scoring, wider baskets) also failed to touch the weighting-escalation basket's rising drawdown, closing that whole avenue.

## Notable failures worth remembering

- **Implementation bug, not a real result (trial #26, `mom_zscore_daily_volspike_trim`):** sparse weight rows weren't zero-filled across the full instrument universe, so forward-fill kept stale weights on names that had dropped from the basket (avg_positions read 132/140 — the tell). Caught via a diagnostic on the weight matrix itself before drawing conclusions, fixed, and re-run cleanly as trial #27. Left in journal/trials history per the never-rewrite-history rule, with a note not to read anything into those numbers.
- **Low-volatility stock tilts are now closed** on this universe, both standalone (val Sharpe 0.69) and as a within-momentum quality filter (val Sharpe 0.64, worse than either alone) — no further variants recommended absent a genuinely different vol construction (e.g. sector-neutralized).
- **Vol targeting/risk parity and regime switching are fully refuted** in every form tried (naive and momentum-combined) — treat as closed families.

# Re-scoring note — 2026-10-05 (protocol v2)

Everything above was measured under protocol v1, on today's ~140 constituents — survivors by
construction. The protocol-v2 re-scoring (merged 2026-10-05, ilasek/trading-autoresearch#4) re-ran
all 104 recorded trials on a point-in-time universe, where a strategy sees a name only while it was
a member of its index (S&P 500 from 1996; eight European and Asian indices from 2009). Across all
trials, v1 and v2 validation rankings agree at Spearman +0.43, none of v1's ten best trials stays in
v2's top ten, and the median validation Sharpe falls from 0.88 to 0.49. Full results:
`reports/protocol-v2-comparison.md` and `reports/protocol-v2-survivorship.md`.

The permanent caveat at the end of the body is therefore no longer hypothetical: the survivorship
bias it describes has now been measured, and for the strategies named in this report it is large.
