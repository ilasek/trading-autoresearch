## Top 3 learnings

1. **Overlay reaction speed must match the speed of what it's trying to catch.** A basket-own realized-vol-spike trim (21d/252d vol ratio) was a near no-op evaluated monthly (fired twice in 6 validation years, zero drawdown improvement) but became the single best risk-reduction lever in the repo once re-evaluated daily — cutting validation maxDD from -36.0% to -29.5%/-30.3% while *raising* Sharpe to a new best of 1.05–1.07. Every earlier de-risking overlay (external trend/regime signals) failed from whipsaw; this one failed from lag until cadence was fixed. General lesson for future overlay ideas: check reaction speed vs. rebalance cadence before concluding a mechanism doesn't work.
2. **Within-basket weighting direction matters as much as membership.** Tilting capital *toward* the strongest-momentum names (rank-weight → z-score-magnitude-weight → multi-horizon z-score) lifted validation Sharpe from 0.90 to 1.03 in three isolated steps — the mirror image of every refuted low-vol/inverse-vol attempt, which tilted away from strong names and lost Sharpe. This is now a well-established, productive lever.
3. **Capital dilution carries a roughly constant Sharpe tax regardless of the base leg's quality**, and de-risking via *switching/blending* (external trend filters, regime gates, hedge-asset redirects, ETF sleeve blends) reliably backfires out-of-sample even when it looks great in-sample — three separate overlay families and a TLT/GLD hedge-redirect attempt all confirmed this. Diversification-axis fixes (sector-neutral scoring, wider baskets) also failed to touch the weighting-escalation basket's rising drawdown, closing that whole avenue.
